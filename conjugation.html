French Grammar

1. Présent

Used for actions happening now, habits and general truths.

Example: Je travaille. — I work / I am working.

2. Passé composé

Used for completed past actions. Formed with the present tense of avoir or être and a past participle.

Example: J'ai fini. — I have finished.

With être, the past participle normally agrees with the subject: Elle est arrivée. With avoir, it normally agrees with a preceding direct object: Les lettres que j'ai écrites.

3. Imparfait

Used for past habits, descriptions and ongoing situations in the past.

Example: Quand j'étais petite, je lisais beaucoup.

4. Plus-que-parfait

Used for an action completed before another past action. Formed with the imparfait of avoir or être and a past participle.

Example: J'avais déjà mangé.

5. Futur simple

Used for future actions and events.

Example: Demain, nous partirons.

6. Conditionnel présent

Used for polite requests, possibilities and hypothetical situations.

Example: Je voudrais un café.

Subject pronouns

Je = I · Tu = You · Il/Elle/On = He/She/One

Nous = We · Vous = You · Ils/Elles = They

Important rules

  • French verbs agree with their subject.
  • Compound tenses use an auxiliary verb: avoir or être.
  • Past participle agreement depends on the auxiliary and sentence structure.
  • Pronominal verbs generally use être in compound tenses.
